Support

Most problems are one of the first three answers below. If none of them fit, email hello@pinme.app and you'll get a reply from a person.

Reminders

A reminder didn't arrive

Work down this list — it's roughly in order of likelihood:

  • Background location isn't granted. Open Settings inside Pin Me and check “Background location”. If it says Off, reminders can only fire while the app is open. On iOS the system permission needs to be “Always”, not “While Using the App”.
  • Notifications are switched off. Also shown in the app's Settings screen. Without it, Pin Me still records the visit but has no way to tell you about it.
  • Reminders are paused. There's a single switch at the top of Settings that pauses everything. It survives restarting the app, so it's easy to forget it's on.
  • You were reminded recently. If a reminder for that place already fired within the last 30 minutes, the next one is suppressed. Standing near the edge of a place would otherwise trigger a burst of notifications.
  • You have more than 20 places. See below.

Why only 20 places at a time?

That's an iOS limit, not ours — the operating system will monitor at most 20 regions per app, and silently ignores any beyond that. Rather than let the system pick arbitrarily, Pin Me watches the 20 places nearest to you and re-picks as you move. The home screen tells you when you've crossed that line. You can save as many places as you like; it only affects which ones are actively armed.

Choosing a radius

The radius is how close you need to get before the reminder fires. Bigger is more reliable: phone positioning is accurate to somewhere between a few metres and a few dozen, and it's worse in dense city streets, indoors, and underground.

  • 50 m — for a specific doorway, when you'll be right on top of it.
  • 100–250 m — the sweet spot for most places. The default is 250 m.
  • 500 m–1 km — for “while I'm in this part of town”, or for a place you drive past rather than walk to.

If you're moving quickly — in a car or on a train — prefer a larger radius. A small circle can be crossed and left again before the operating system gets around to noticing.

The reminder came at the wrong moment

Geofence timing is handled by the operating system, and it trades precision for battery life. A notification can arrive a minute or two after you actually arrive. That's normal and not something the app can override.

Battery and data

Will this drain my battery?

It shouldn't be noticeable. Pin Me doesn't poll your location or keep GPS running. It hands your places to the operating system's own geofencing service, which is built for exactly this and uses low-power signals — cell towers and Wi-Fi — most of the time.

Does it use mobile data?

Only for map imagery when you have the map open. Reminders themselves need no connection at all and work perfectly in airplane mode.

Your data

Where are my places stored?

On your phone, and nowhere else. There's no account and no server. The privacy policy spells this out in full.

How do I delete everything?

Settings → Delete all data removes every place and the entire reminder history. It can't be undone. Uninstalling the app has the same effect.

Will my places move to a new phone?

Only if your new phone is set up from a device backup that includes app data. There's no sync built into Pin Me, so a fresh install starts empty.

Can I see the introduction again?

Yes — Settings → Show the intro again. Your saved places aren't touched.

Still stuck?

Email hello@pinme.app. It helps if you mention your phone model, your OS version, and what you expected to happen instead.

Bug reports and feature requests are both welcome. Pin Me is made by one person, so replies may take a couple of days — but they do arrive.
